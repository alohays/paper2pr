# SUNY Speaker Notes Audience Impact Review

Audience reviewed against: STEM undergraduates at SUNY Korea, with approximately 20% international students.

Overall pattern: the notes are strong on credibility, urgency, and practical value. The main weakness is sustained engagement in the middle of the talk, where several slides become dense monologues instead of audience-involving beats.

### Slide 1: Cover
Impact: 4/5 | Engagement: 3/5
Strengths: Establishes warmth, authority, and the wave metaphor clearly.
Weaknesses: The opening is polite rather than instantly gripping; the first sentence does not yet create tension.
Suggestion: Open with the threat/opportunity first, then introduce yourself after the room is leaning in.

### Slide 2: Who Am I?
Impact: 5/5 | Engagement: 4/5
Strengths: "Licensed bartender" is a memorable surprise and makes the speaker feel human, not distant.
Weaknesses: Runs a little long before landing the audience-facing takeaway.
Suggestion: Trim one credential beat and pause longer on the bartender-to-research-lead contrast.

### Slide 3: The Wave Is Here
Impact: 3/5 | Engagement: 2/5
Strengths: Gives a clean roadmap and frames the talk structure.
Weaknesses: Too explanatory for a section-divider slide; the opening is generic and the energy dips right after Slide 2.
Suggestion: Turn this into a short promise statement, not a full outline narration.

### Slide 4: AI Revolution by the Numbers
Impact: 4/5 | Engagement: 3/5
Strengths: The 200M comparison is strong and the Karpathy risk framing adds urgency.
Weaknesses: The first half is stat-heavy without a direct audience prompt.
Suggestion: Ask for a quick show of hands from students planning software careers before revealing the risk score.

### Slide 5: The AI Job Map
Impact: 3/5 | Engagement: 2/5
Strengths: Useful orientation for confused students who only know "AI" as one bucket.
Weaknesses: Reads like a quadrant walkthrough instead of a moment of choice; not very memorable.
Suggestion: Frame it as "Which wave are you actually training for?" and invite students to pick a quadrant mentally.

### Slide 6: Salary Reality Check
Impact: 4/5 | Engagement: 2/5
Strengths: Honest, relevant, and likely to matter to undergraduates immediately.
Weaknesses: Long for a chart slide and mostly one-way explanation.
Suggestion: Lead with the Korea-vs-US gap as a shock line, then interpret it in fewer sentences.

### Slide 7: The Paradox
Impact: 5/5 | Engagement: 4/5
Strengths: Strongest early-slide tension; the contradiction is easy to understand and hard to forget.
Weaknesses: Slightly overloaded with supporting facts near the end.
Suggestion: Keep the impossible contradiction front and center, and trim one supporting stat for speed.

### Slide 8: What Recruiters Actually Want
Impact: 4/5 | Engagement: 3/5
Strengths: Highly relevant and reassuring for students without elite credentials.
Weaknesses: The note explains both sides well but does not force a self-reflection moment.

### Slide 9: Physical AI: The Next Wave
Impact: 5/5 | Engagement: 3/5
Strengths: Big-future energy, strong speaker credibility, and a clear Korea angle.
Weaknesses: It inspires more than it interacts; the note would benefit from a sharper personal stake for the audience.
Suggestion: Ask who has considered robotics or embodied AI before revealing Korea's positioning.

## Overall Impact Score

Overall impact score: 82/100

Interpretation: the talk is already strong enough to move students emotionally and practically, but it is being held back by long low-interaction stretches in the middle. Credibility and clarity are strong; engagement density is the main limiter.

Closing punch rating:
- Slide 60: 3/5
- Slide 61: 5/5
- Slide 62: 5/5

Assessment: the ending lands well because Slides 61 and 62 recover the emotional arc after Slide 60's logistical dip. Slide 62 is the right final move.

## Top 10 Highest-Impact Notes

- Slide 2: bartender-to-research-lead story is instantly human and memorable.
- Slide 7: the paradox creates the strongest early tension in the deck.
- Slide 15: the 5M-user scale is simple, concrete, and impressive.
- Slide 20: vulnerability plus lesson makes this one of the most trustworthy notes.
- Slide 27: persistence from 29% to 97.3% is a strong emotional and technical story.
- Slide 28: game-data-to-robot learning has genuine wow factor.
- Slide 37: the GPA-versus-portfolio challenge is highly audience-relevant.
- Slide 40: the "homework won't cut it" challenge is blunt and memorable.
- Slide 61: the core metaphor returns with real urgency.
- Slide 62: strongest engagement design in the talk; it forces action before applause.

## Top 10 Notes Needing Most Improvement

- Slide 3: too long and too generic for an early section divider.
- Slide 5: useful information, but low memorability and low audience involvement.
- Slide 11: jargon-heavy and overly dense for this audience.
- Slide 21: important lesson buried inside a long column walkthrough.
- Slide 25: abstract three-pillar explanation weakens momentum entering Part 3.
- Slide 30: stats-first structure hides the stronger public-resume insight.
- Slide 31: dense infrastructure explanation becomes emotionally flat.
- Slide 41: too many project examples dilute the call to action.
- Slide 47: interview pyramid is useful but delivered in a low-impact way.
- Slide 50: year-by-year roadmap is practical but too evenly narrated to stick.

## Specific Rewrite Suggestions For The 5 Weakest Notes

- Slide 3:
  Rewrite direction: "This wave is not coming after you graduate. It is already here. Tonight I want to show you where it is, how I caught it twice, and what you need to surf it before the market decides for you."

- Slide 11:
  Rewrite direction: "Last year AI helped you write code. This year AI expects you to manage teammates that happen to be machines. That shift is what people mean by agentic engineering."

- Slide 25:
  Rewrite direction: "My job now is simple to say and hard to do: make robots work outside PowerPoint. At WoRV, we do that through three linked bets: navigation, manipulation, and simulation."

- Slide 47:
  Rewrite direction: "Interviews do not reject you at the top of the pyramid. They reject you at the first weak layer. So ask yourself: if your interview were tomorrow, which layer would fail first?"

- Slide 50:
  Rewrite direction: "Whatever year you are in, you only have one job right now. Year 1: explore. Year 2: build. Year 3: ship. Year 4: land. Do not memorize all four. Remember yours."
