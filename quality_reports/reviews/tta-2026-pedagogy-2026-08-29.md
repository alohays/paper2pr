## Summary

- **Deck-level assessment:** A strong, visually-driven survey talk with a clear spine (VLA recipe -> world models -> WAM merger -> in-context-learning payoff -> scoreboard). Visual-first execution is exemplary: nearly every concept gets footage before or with its text, and every definition is followed by a concrete exemplar within one slide. The two genuine weaknesses are narrative, not structural: (1) the driving problem - teleop data is the bottleneck - is promised by divider 01 ("where it strains") but never stated on any slide, so the entire second half answers a question the audience was never asked; (2) zero fragment reveals means the deck's three best punchlines (66% vs 9%, "hybrid will dominate", the China "still not enough" row) fire with no build-up. Both are one-line fixes. Timing is feasible but concentrated: slides 26-34 carry six dense tables in the back half of a 22-minute speaking budget.

## Pattern-by-Pattern Assessment

### 1. Motivation before formalism - PARTIAL (Medium-High)
- **Evidence:** Each section opens with a one-line definition slide (slide 4 "A VLA is a vision-language model...", slide 10 "A world model learns what happens next", slide 19 "A WAM denoises video and action together"). These are well-calibrated for practitioners. But the motivating problem for the whole arc - teleoperation data is expensive, scarce, and embodiment-locked - is never stated. Divider 01's subtitle promises "The VLA recipe, and where it strains," yet no slide in section 01 names the strain. Slide 7 ("Every VLA stands on human demonstration") shows the data engine neutrally; slide 8 jumps to the cure ("VLAs began absorbing world models") without the disease.
- **Recommendation:** One added line (or fragment) on slide 7: something like "the ceiling: teleop hours are costly and do not transfer across embodiments." Then slide 8, section 02, and the entire WAM/human-video thesis become answers to a stated question, and "If you remember one slide" (35) ties back to an opening tension instead of just summarizing.

### 2. Incremental notation - FOLLOWED (Low note)
- **Evidence:** The deck is nearly notation-free, which is right for this room. The only formula, $\log N \propto 1/(P-c)$, appears in a footnote on slide 26 and again in A7 with N, P, c all undefined.
- **Recommendation:** See Challenge 5. In the main deck, render it in plain English; keep the formula in A7 with a one-line gloss of the symbols.

### 3. Worked example after every definition - FOLLOWED
- **Evidence:** VLA definition (4) -> timeline (5) -> Helix/ALOHA/UMI footage (6-7). World model definition (10) -> three-families table (11) -> Genie 3 video (12). WAM definition (19) -> DreamZero video (20). No definition sits more than one slide from a concrete instance. This is the deck's strongest structural habit.

### 4. Progressive complexity - PARTIAL (Medium)
- **Evidence:** The macro-ordering is exactly right: single-model VLA -> dual-system -> world models -> joint WAM -> in-context learning -> comparison. Two wrinkles: (a) slide 8's chip says "visual subgoals from a lightweight world model" two slides before "world model" is defined (slide 10) - literally an advanced concept before its prerequisite, though defensible as a section-closing teaser for practitioners; (b) the System 1 / System 2 framing introduced on slide 6 is never used again, even though slide 23's footnote ("recent WAMs skip video generation and act at VLA-class latency") is the natural payoff for it.
- **Recommendation:** Add a three-word gloss to the slide 8 chip or bridge it verbally in the notes; consider one clause on slide 23 connecting back to the 7-9 Hz / 200 Hz split from slide 6.

### 5. Fragment reveals for problem -> solution - VIOLATED (Medium)
- **Evidence:** Zero `. . .` fragments in 46 slides (target 3-5 per deck). The deck has several natural problem->solution beats that currently fire all at once: slide 26's table ends on the China row ("90 data factories... still not enough") plus the Curse-of-Precision footnote - a reveal built for two beats; slide 31 poses "One human video vs 380 teleop episodes" and shows the 66%-vs-9% answer simultaneously; slide 34's footnote drops "hybrid will dominate" in the same breath as the 5x4 table.
- **Recommendation:** Add 3-4 fragments at exactly these punchlines: slide 26 (table, then footnote), slide 31 (videos, then chips), slide 34 (table, then footnote), optionally slide 17 (the "two halves of one loop" footnote).

### 6. Standout slides at conceptual pivots - FOLLOWED (Low note)

- **Recommendation (cosmetic):** Slide 27 carries no `.divider-number` while sitting between 03 and 04, leaving it ambiguous whether the ICL wave is part of section 03 or its own act. Either number it (03.5 / 04 and renumber) or accept the asymmetry deliberately.

### 7. Two-slide strategy for dense material - FOLLOWED
- **Evidence:** Dyna-2 gets hero video (24) -> deployment economics (25) -> appendix detail (A1, A2). DreamZero/DreamDojo get video (16, 20) plus the comparison table (17). The dense scoreboard (34) is immediately distilled into "If you remember one slide" (35) - the two-slide strategy run in reverse, which works. ### 8. Semantic color usage - PARTIAL (Low-Medium)

But every binary contrast - "95 vs 35" (25), "66% vs 9%" (31), "89% vs 42%" (A3), "+62%" (29) - renders both sides in the same chip style with no visual marking of which side wins. The pattern's red flag ("binary contrasts shown in the same color") applies.

### 9. Box hierarchy - FOLLOWED

## Deck-Level Analysis

**Narrative arc.** Coherent and well-shaped: recipe (01) -> the environment itself (02) -> the merger (03) -> the payoff (03.5, ICL) -> scoreboard (04) -> team + QR. The subtitle's promise ("what changed by August 2026") is answered explicitly by slide 35. Two soft spots: the unstated strain (Pattern 1), and the WoRV/CostNav close (36-37), which connects to the thesis only through one chip ("RFM plus a world-model research track") - acceptable for an invited talk's home-team segment, but one spoken sentence linking WoRV's data engine to the "hours, not parameters" argument would make it feel earned rather than appended.

**Pacing (against speaking_min 22, not the 40-minute wall clock).** Full-video slides consume 6.4 of the 8 video minutes - within budget, with the 1.6-minute slack realistically absorbed by card slides where footage plays under narration. That leaves ~23 speaking-load slides for 22 minutes, ~55-60 s each: feasible, but not uniform. Six table slides (11, 15, 17, 23, 26, 28) plus the scoreboard (34) each want 60-90 s, and five of them sit in the back half. The single biggest media block is dreamzero-overview at 122.5 s - if it runs in full, that is a quarter of the entire video budget on one slide; decide in rehearsal whether to trim it or narrate over it. Dyna2-hero (102.3 s) is the same question. The appendix strategy is correct: none of A1-A8 should be presented in the main flow.

**Visual rhythm.** Excellent. The longest run of non-video slides is two (13-15 is card/text/table, then video; 22-23 is text/table, then video). Dense -> footage -> dense alternation holds through all four sections.

**Notation consistency.** Three small issues. (a) Pi subscripting: the timeline (5) uses proper subscripts (&pi;<sub>0</sub>) while chips and tables elsewhere use plain "&pi;0.5" / "&pi;0.7" (20, 34) - pick one rendering. (b) "GR 2" (slide 8 chip) is ambiguous: presumably Gemini Robotics 2, but NVIDIA's GR00T appears in this same deck (A3, "GR00T N1.7"), and "GR" is the collision-prone prefix in this field. (c) "DYNA-1" (A2) vs "Dyna-2" vs "Dyna Robotics" casing varies - likely faithful to vendor branding, but worth a deliberate check. Elo, TTT, and ZFLOPs go unexpanded, which `expand_acronyms: false` permits and a practitioner room absorbs; TTT (slide 29 chip) is the only one I would gloss verbally.

**Pre-empting audience concerns.** Good instincts: Genie's physics gaps are "self-stated" (15), the Curse of Precision is presented as the counterweight to the data race (26), and NVIDIA's own "inference 3 to 4x slower" caveat is kept (34). What is missing for this particular room: nearly every headline number (Dyna-2's 95 vs 35, GEN-1.5's 59%, Skild's 66% vs 9%) is a vendor-blog claim, not peer-reviewed. The deck attributes sources honestly, but a standards-body audience deserves one explicit sentence - spoken or footnoted on slide 28 - that these are company-reported figures on company-chosen tasks. That caveat costs nothing and buys credibility for everything else.

**Delivery readiness observation.** `notes: ko` is declared but the QMD contains zero `::: {.notes}` blocks. With a 22-minute budget, dense back-half tables, and two long videos that may need talking over, Korean speaker notes with per-section time marks are the single highest-leverage remaining artifact. (If notes are produced in a later pass per the repo workflow, this is already planned - flagging so it is not forgotten.)

**Forbidden-terms spot check (pedagogy-adjacent).** The deck stays clear of the five forbidden strings (consortium, Yangjae, first in Korea, WoRV-1, flagship-rfm); the WoRV segment describes only public material (RFM, CostNav, hiring). Good discipline for the profile's leak-prone genre.

## Challenges

### Challenge 1: Gaps - State the strain before selling the cure
**Question:** Divider 01 promises "The VLA recipe, and where it strains" - where, on an actual slide, does it strain?
**Why it matters:** The entire second half of the talk (world models as data engines, WAMs trained on human video, the hours race, ICL) is the answer to "teleop data is the bottleneck." That problem is never stated. A practitioner who half-knows the field will infer it; anyone else experiences sections 02-03 as a parade of capabilities without a reason. The conclusion (35) then summarizes rather than resolves.
**Suggested resolution:** One added line or fragment on slide 7: "The ceiling: teleop hours are expensive, slow to collect, and locked to one embodiment." Slide 8's "VLAs began absorbing world models" then lands as a response, and the human-video thesis of section 03 becomes inevitable rather than asserted.
**Slides affected:** 3 (divider 01), 7, 8; payoff at 35.
**Severity:** High

### Challenge 2: Ordering - "world model" used two slides before it is defined
**Question:** Slide 8's chip credits pi0.7 with "visual subgoals from a lightweight world model"; the term is defined on slide 10. Should the definition come first, or should the chip carry a gloss?
**Why it matters:** For the declared practitioner room this is survivable, but it is exactly the pattern-4 red flag, and slide 8 is a full-bleed video slide where the audience is reading chips cold, with no narration anchor if timing slips.
**Suggested resolution:** Cheapest fix: reword the chip to "visual subgoals imagined by a small predictive model, at inference," letting slide 10 formally name the concept. Alternative: accept it as a deliberate teaser and script the bridge in the (currently absent) speaker notes: "hold that phrase - next section defines it."
**Slides affected:** 8, 10.
**Severity:** Medium

### Challenge 3: Cognitive load - the scoreboard slide carries three arguments at once
**Question:** Slide 34 is a 5x4 table (five architectures, four attributes) plus a footnote making three separate claims (9 vs 50+ ZFLOPs, 3-4x inference gap closing, "hybrid will dominate"). Can one slide carry the taxonomy, the compute economics, and the prediction?
**Why it matters:** This is the intellectual climax of the talk and the slide most likely to be photographed. Read all at once, the footnote's "hybrid will dominate" - the single most quotable claim in the deck - is buried under a table the audience is still parsing. ZFLOPs also appears here for the first and only time.
**Suggested resolution:** Fragment the slide: table first (talk through the rows), then reveal the footnote as its own beat. Or move the NVIDIA compute claim to a spoken line and keep only "hybrid will dominate" as the reveal. Slide 35 already exists as the distilled version, so 34 can afford to shed weight.
**Slides affected:** 34, 35.
**Severity:** Medium

### Challenge 4: Notation conflicts - "GR 2" in a deck that also contains GR00T
**Question:** Does the audience read "GR 2" (slide 8 chip) as Gemini Robotics 2 - given that NVIDIA GR00T N1.7 appears in the same deck (A3) and "GR" is the ambiguous prefix of this field?
**Why it matters:** A listener who mis-binds "GR 2" to GR00T walks away attributing DeepMind's under-200-demo humanoid adaptation to NVIDIA. On a video-full slide with 0.42em chip text, there is no room for context to disambiguate. `expand_acronyms: false` permits abbreviations, but not within-deck ambiguity.
**Suggested resolution:** Write "Gemini Robotics 2" in the chip (it fits), or at minimum "GDM GR 2." Sources list confirms the DeepMind blog is the referent.
**Slides affected:** 8; collision with A3.
**Severity:** Medium

### Challenge 5: Prerequisites - an undefined power law in a footnote, twice
**Question:** Does this room parse $\log N \propto 1/(P-c)$ on slide 26's footnote when N, P, and c are never defined anywhere in the deck?
**Why it matters:** The Curse of Precision is the deck's only counterweight to the data-race narrative - arguably its most intellectually honest moment - and it is delivered as an unexplained formula in footnote type. The audience either ignores it (losing the counterargument) or stalls on it (losing the next slide).
**Suggested resolution:** Main deck: plain English - "required data grows explosively as precision demands approach the model's limit; precision is the one axis volume does not buy." Keep the formula in A7 with a one-line symbol gloss (N = demonstrations needed, P = required precision, c = the model's precision floor) for Q&A.
**Slides affected:** 26 (footnote), A7.
**Severity:** Medium

### Challenge 6: Standalone value - the QR handout is a deck of empty video frames
**Question:** Slide 38 invites the room to revisit the deck via QR. Seven video-full slides and 13 cards are the deck's argument - what does a reader see if the export or a slow connection drops the footage?
**Why it matters:** Slides 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 30, 36 reduce to floating chips over a blank background without their video; the ICL section (30-32) loses its entire evidentiary content. The video-caption shortcodes and poster files in the lock suggest the framework handles this, but it is worth one explicit check because the QR slide makes standalone reading a promised use case.
**Suggested resolution:** Verify the HTML-on-phone and export_pdf paths render poster frames plus captions for every video-full and card; if any poster is missing from the release, that is the fix.
**Slides affected:** all `.video-full` slides and two-up card slides; promise made on 38.
**Severity:** Low-Medium

### Challenge 7: Alternative presentation - let the punchline numbers win visually
**Question:** "66% vs 9%" (31), "95 vs 35" (25), "89% vs 42%" (A3): both numbers wear the same chip style. Two alternatives - (a) fragment the reveal: question and videos first, chips on the click; (b) semantic weighting: winning number in the gold/navy accent, baseline in gray.
**Why it matters:** These deltas are the talk's emotional evidence; rendered symmetrically, the audience does the comparison work themselves at reading speed instead of feeling it at reveal speed.
**Suggested resolution:** Option (b) is a pure SCSS change to the chip idiom and covers every instance at once; option (a) on slide 31 alone would also satisfy Pattern 5. Doing (b) globally plus (a) on 31 is the best value.
**Slides affected:** 25, 29, 31, 32, A3.
**Severity:** Low

## Challenge Verdict
**Strengths:** (1) Visual-first execution is the best I have seen in this repo's talk genre - every definition earns footage within one slide, and the video budget is actually respected (6.4 of 8 min). (2) The comparison architecture (17, 34, 35) gives the audience durable take-home structure; "If you remember one slide" is exactly the one-repeatable-claim discipline the invited-talk profile asks for. (3) Honest caveats survive the hype: Genie's self-stated physics gaps, the Curse of Precision, NVIDIA's own latency caveat.
**Critical changes:** (1) State the teleop-bottleneck strain on slide 7 (Challenge 1) - one line that converts the second half from catalog to argument. (2) Disambiguate "GR 2" on slide 8 (Challenge 4) - a mis-attribution in a photographed slide is the costliest small error available.
**Suggested improvements:** (1) Add 3-4 fragments at the punchlines (26, 31, 34). (2) Plain-English the Curse of Precision in the main deck, formula to appendix. (3) Write the Korean speaker notes with time marks before rehearsal - the 22-minute budget with six back-half tables will not survive improvisation.
