## Pattern-by-Pattern Assessment

### 1. Motivation before formalism - PARTIAL (Medium)

**Evidence:** The whole-deck motivation is now present: slide 7's footnote states that one hour of robot time buys one hour of data for one embodiment, and its notes explicitly describe the teleoperation ceiling. That gives slides 8-35 a real problem to solve. World models are motivated by the pi0.7 transition on slide 8 before their definition on slide 10; WAMs are motivated by the DreamZero/DreamDojo forward-versus-inverse comparison on slide 17 before their definition on slide 19. The remaining inversion is the opening baseline: slide 4 formally defines VLA before slides 5-7 establish why that recipe matters and where it strains.

**Recommendation:** Keep the order, which is defensible for practitioners, but put the driving question visibly on divider 01 or as a short line on slide 4: "What breaks when progress requires one more hour of teleoperation for every hour of data?" This would make the opening conform to Why -> What without adding a slide.

### 2. Incremental notation - PARTIAL (Low-Medium)

**Evidence:** The main story is deliberately light on mathematical notation. VLA is expanded on slide 4, world model is defined on slide 10, and WAM is defined on slide 19 before the denser taxonomy on slide 23. That progression suits `practitioner`. The exception is appendix slide A7, where `log N proportional to 1/(P-c)` appears with `N`, `P`, and `c` undefined. Display also varies between proper pi subscripts on slide 5 and plain `pi0.7`/`pi0.5` in slides 8, 20, and 34.

**Recommendation:** Define the three symbols in A7 or replace the equation with its plain-English consequence. Normalize the pi model typography. No additional main-deck formalism is needed.

### 3. Worked example after every definition - FOLLOWED

**Evidence:** VLA (slide 4) is followed by the model timeline and Helix demonstration (5-6). The world-model definition (10) is followed by its three families (11) and the Genie 3 example (12), exactly two slides later. The WAM definition (19) is followed immediately by DreamZero (20). Latent/pseudo-actions are introduced with LAPA footage on slide 21; the joint-model mechanism on slide 22 is followed by the three-style taxonomy (23) and Dyna-2 demonstration (24). There are no consecutive formal-definition slides without an example.

### 4. Progressive complexity - PARTIAL (Medium)

**Evidence:** The macro-sequence is strong: direct imitation -> dual-system VLA -> predictive world model -> neural simulator -> WAM -> action supervision -> deployment scale -> in-context learning -> comparison. The one prerequisite inversion is slide 8: "visual subgoals from a lightweight world model" appears before slide 10 explains what a world model is. A second, smaller step-up occurs on slide 23, which introduces "flow," "mixture-of-transformers," and "latent world + decoder" together after the simpler joint-denoising diagram; a practitioner can follow the categories but may not know what operational difference each makes.

**Recommendation:** Treat slide 8 explicitly as a teaser ("a predictive model imagines visual subgoals; next, we name it") and add one plain-English consequence to each slide-23 row rather than another mechanism term.

### 5. Fragment reveals for problem -> solution - VIOLATED (Medium)

**Evidence:** There are zero `. . .` fragments against the 3-5 target. **Recommendation:** Use three deliberate reveals only: slide 21 question -> pseudo-action/latent-action answers; slide 31 human demonstration -> robot execution -> numerical exchange rate; slide 34 taxonomy -> cost/latency caveat. ## Deck-Level Analysis

### Narrative arc

The main story is coherent and cumulative:

1. Slides 3-8 establish the VLA recipe, its teleoperation dependency, and the first VLA/world-model bridge.
2. Slides 9-17 define world models, show their convergence on robot training, and create the forward/inverse bridge to WAM.
3. Slides 18-26 define WAM, explain its supervision and architecture, then show scaling and deployment.
4. Slides 27-32 answer what scale buys: in-context task specification from human video.
5. Slides 33-35 compare the architectures and restate the thesis.

The conclusion on slide 35 directly answers the subtitle: behavior/dynamics/both; teleop/video/egocentric video; and the 2026 convergence. The visible landing then weakens slightly. Slides 36-37 move into WoRV and CostNav after the "one slide to remember," so the last substantive image before the QR is company research rather than the data-axis thesis. The QR notes restate the thesis, but the projected QR slide shows only "Thank you. Questions welcome." For an invited talk, a short team coda is legitimate; pedagogically, the final thesis should remain visible on the QR slide or slide 35 should be repeated after CostNav.

### Pacing: 22 spoken minutes

The script is quantitatively on budget: 6,237 Hangul syllables versus a 6,160 target. The longest theory-heavy runs also respect the 3-4-slide ceiling: world-model definition/families are followed by Genie (10-12); slides 13-15 move from a video card through industry evidence before DreamDojo (16); slides 21-23 move from LAPA through mechanism/taxonomy before Dyna-2 (24); slides 28-29 are followed by GEN-1.5 (30). The appendix correctly carries detail for Q&A and should not enter the timed main run.

The operational risk is video, not prose. Fifteen playback slides would occupy 10.0 minutes at full asset duration, two minutes beyond `video_min: 8`. DreamZero (122.5 s) and Dyna-2 (102.3 s) alone consume 3.75 minutes; Genie 3 (51.9 s) and LAPA (51.7 s) add another 1.73. Because the 22-minute prose budget is already full, the presenter needs a cue sheet with intended watch windows or trimmed manifests. A safe rehearsal gate is: 22 minutes spoken, no more than 8 minutes of actual silent playback, and the QR visible by 30:00 so the promised 10-minute Q&A remains intact.

### Visual rhythm

Rhythm is a strength. Five main dividers alternate diagrams, tables, videos, and two-up demonstrations. No main section stays abstract for more than three slides. Full-bleed video functions as a breather and as evidence, not decoration. The repeated chip idiom is the only source of visual monotony: the WAM/ICL back half uses three-chip groups on slides 20, 24, 25, 29, 30, 31, and 32. Varying just the climax slides into one focal metric plus subordinate text would restore hierarchy without changing the overall design language.

### Notation and conceptual consistency

VLA, world model, and WAM are introduced in a sensible order and used consistently as labels. Four issues remain:

- Slide 19 defines WAM categorically as joint video/action denoising, while slide 23 includes interleaved mixtures and latent-world-plus-decoder models. The audience is left unsure whether slide 19 is the definition or one implementation.
- Slide 23 says recent WAMs skip video generation and act at "VLA-class latency"; slide 34 says WAM inference is still `3 to 4x slower and closing`. These can both be true for different models or pathways, but no qualifier tells the audience that.
- Pi model typography alternates between subscripted and plain forms.
- Appendix A7's only equation leaves all symbols undefined.

The first two are conceptual contradictions from the audience's perspective and should be resolved before presentation; the latter two are cleanup.

### Audience preparation and pre-empted concerns

For `practitioner`, the deck appropriately defines the three headline model classes and avoids unnecessary equations. It also acknowledges two important counterweights: the Curse of Precision on slide 26, and WAM compute/inference cost on slide 34. Deployment evidence (slide 25) and the CostNav unit-economics example (37) help a practitioner room connect research to operations.

The weakest prerequisite slide is 23: `flow`, `mixture-of-transformers`, `latent world`, and `decoder` are presented as taxonomy labels without a plain-English consequence. More importantly, a TTA-facing practitioner may ask questions the deck does not yet pre-empt: Are vendor-reported success rates comparable? What action representation or embodiment interface is shared? How are human-video provenance, consent, and licensing handled? What safety/evaluation evidence distinguishes a compelling demo from a deployable system? The deck need not answer all four in 22 minutes, but one standards-facing final slide or one directed Q&A prompt should name the decision surface. That would turn the room from spectators into participants.

## Challenges

### Challenge 1: Gaps - Is joint denoising the definition or one implementation?
**Question:** Slide 19 says "A WAM denoises video and action together," but slide 23 admits joint denoising, interleaved mixture, and latent-world-plus-decoder styles. What property actually defines the category?
**Why it matters:** A practitioner may leave with a brittle rule that excludes two of the three architectures shown four slides later. The title-level definition carries more authority than the later taxonomy.
**Suggested resolution:** Define WAM broadly on slide 19 as a model that learns world prediction and action in one training system; label joint denoising as the canonical example in the diagram. Then slide 23 becomes a set of implementations rather than a correction.
**Slides affected:** 19, 22, 23, 34-35
**Severity:** High

### Challenge 2: Notation conflicts - Which latency claim should the audience retain?
**Question:** Are recent WAMs at VLA-class latency (slide 23), or are they still 3-4x slower (slide 34)?
**Why it matters:** Latency is a deployment decision for this audience. An unexplained reversal near the conclusion undermines confidence in the otherwise clean comparison.
**Suggested resolution:** Qualify both claims by model/pathway: for example, "action-only WAM pathways can approach VLA latency; full generative WAM inference remains roughly 3-4x slower in NVIDIA's June estimate." Use the same wording on both slides.
**Slides affected:** 23, 34, appendix A1-A2 if relevant
**Severity:** High

### Challenge 3: Cognitive load - Can the declared video budget survive full assets?
**Question:** Which exact moments are intended to play on the 15 playback slides, given a 10.0-minute full-play ceiling and `video_min: 8`?
**Why it matters:** The Korean script already fills the 22-minute speech budget. Two extra playback minutes consume Q&A, and the longest clips (DreamZero 122.5 s; Dyna-2 102.3 s) can silently distort section balance.
**Suggested resolution:** Put cue-out timestamps in the presenter script and rehearse a hard 8-minute playback total. If the intended excerpts cannot be cued reliably in Reveal, trim the two longest assets to the moments actually discussed.
**Slides affected:** especially 20 and 24; also 12, 21, and all other video slides
**Severity:** High

### Challenge 4: Prerequisites - The teaser uses "world model" before teaching it
**Question:** Could slide 8's pi0.7 example describe a "predictive model that imagines visual subgoals" before slide 10 formally names a world model?
**Why it matters:** A teaser can create curiosity, but the full-bleed slide asks the audience to read three claims while watching motion. An undefined core term at that moment raises avoidable load.
**Suggested resolution:** Either use the plain-English gloss on slide 8 or add a spoken forward pointer: "That predictive module is the world model we define next."
**Slides affected:** 8, 9, 10
**Severity:** Medium

### Challenge 5: Standalone value - What should this practitioner room do with the trend?
**Question:** After slide 35 establishes the shift from teleoperation to human video, what is the room's actionable decision: standardize data provenance, action interfaces, evaluation, or something else?
**Why it matters:** The deck ends its intellectual argument, then moves to WoRV/CostNav and a generic Q&A invitation. In a TTA PG1004 context, the audience may admire the trend without knowing why it matters to their work.
**Suggested resolution:** Two viable alternatives: (a) replace CostNav slide 37 with "Three standardization questions for WAMs"; or (b) keep CostNav and add one directed question to the QR slide. If the TTA inference is out of scope, keep the existing close and treat this as optional.
**Slides affected:** 35-38
**Severity:** Medium-High (conditional on the TTA context)

### Challenge 6: Alternative presentation - Let the scoreboard unfold instead of arriving complete
**Question:** Would slide 34 work better as taxonomy first, then cost/latency caveat, then the hybrid prediction?
**Why it matters:** Five architecture rows, four comparison columns, two Elo values, and a three-part footnote compete at the conceptual climax. With no fragments, the audience reads ahead while the presenter is still establishing the categories.
**Suggested resolution:** Option 1: reveal the WAM and Hybrid rows after the baseline rows, then reveal the caveat. Option 2: show slide 35 first as the mental model and keep slide 34 as an appendix-grade evidence table. In either option, visually accent focal values and mute baselines.
**Slides affected:** 34, 35
**Severity:** Medium

### Challenge 7: Standalone value - The appendix equation has no key
**Question:** Can a QR reader interpret `log N proportional to 1/(P-c)` on A7 without definitions of `N`, `P`, and `c`?
**Why it matters:** The appendix is explicitly the promised Q&A/handout detail. Undefined symbols make the deck's most important counterargument to unlimited data scaling less usable precisely where detail is expected.
**Suggested resolution:** Add a one-line symbol key in A7 and use only the plain-English consequence in the main deck: precision demands cause data needs to grow explosively near the model's limit.
**Slides affected:** 26, A7
**Severity:** Low-Medium

## Challenge Verdict

**Strengths:** The deck has a clear causal spine, exceptionally frequent concrete demonstrations, and comparison slides (17, 34, 35) that give practitioners a durable mental model. Its notes are now quantitatively aligned to the 22-minute speech budget. It also keeps meaningful caveats rather than presenting an uninterrupted hype narrative.
**Critical changes:** Reconcile the WAM category definition and latency claims across slides 19/23/34. Establish and rehearse explicit playback cue-outs so the 8-minute video allocation is real rather than aspirational.
**Suggested improvements:** Add three purposeful fragment sequences; give the TTA-context audience one actionable standards question; define the appendix equation and normalize comparison styling/notation.

## Critical Recommendations (Top 5)

1. **Resolve the two conceptual inconsistencies before presenting:** broad WAM definition versus implementation on slides 19/23, and VLA-class versus 3-4x latency on slides 23/34. These affect what the audience believes, not just slide polish.
2. **Lock the playback plan to 8 minutes:** cue or trim DreamZero and Dyna-2 first, then rehearse QR-on-screen by minute 30 to protect the full 10-minute Q&A.
3. **Make the final landing actionable for the inferred TTA context:** keep the thesis visible after the WoRV/CostNav coda and seed one concrete standards question.
4. **Add exactly three problem-solution reveals:** slides 21, 31, and 34. This satisfies the fragment pattern and improves the three highest-value explanation beats.
5. **Clean up the reference layer:** define `N`, `P`, and `c` in A7; normalize pi model typography; accent focal values and mute baselines in binary comparisons.
