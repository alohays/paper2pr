## Mismatches

### Issue 1: The deck presents one WAM subtype as the definition of every WAM

- **Slides:** `A WAM denoises video and action together`; `Five architectures, one scoreboard`
- **Status / severity:** **MISMATCH — CRITICAL**
- **Slide says:** “A WAM denoises video and action together”; the scoreboard says all WAM actions come from “joint denoising.”
- **Sources say:** S09 explicitly organizes WAMs into “inverse dynamics vs joint prediction vs representation-only” and explains that only a joint-prediction WAM “emits video and actions together.” S25 gives four representative implementations: explicit future generation, predictive-video features, joint video-action generation, and auxiliary future prediction. S27 likewise contrasts joint-modeling, causal imagine-then-act, and Fast-WAM.
- **Additional slide claim:** “Human video is no longer pretraining: it is action supervision” and the diagram states “video prediction = action supervision.” S05 says the opposite boundary precisely: “a human video does not provide executable robot actions”; its paired robot trajectory retains those actions. S01 derives *pseudo-actions* from extracted 3D hand-pose tracks, and S12/S23 uses about 500 hours of action-labeled robot teleoperation.
- **Why it matters:** this is the deck's central technical definition, not a peripheral simplification. A practitioner audience would leave believing WAM means a monolithic joint diffusion architecture and that video prediction itself supplies motor labels.
- **Narrowest fix:** retitle the slide **“A joint-prediction WAM denoises video and action together”**; change the scoreboard row to “joint or video-conditioned action pathway”; change the supervision line to “human video supplies dynamics and pseudo-action supervision; executable actions still require pseudo-labels or robot trajectories.”

### Issue 2: DreamZero is labeled inverse-dynamics even though its declared sources say it has no inverse-dynamics module

- **Slide:** `Two paradigms, one team`
- **Status / severity:** **MISMATCH — MAJOR**
- **Slide says:** DreamZero direction is “inverse: video to action.”
- **Source says:** S09 calls DreamZero “a joint-prediction WAM” and states: “There is no separate inverse-dynamics module: action is another generated modality inside the same denoising process.” Its comparison table classifies DreamZero as `Joint Prediction`. S12 also says it jointly predicts world states and actions.
- **Fix:** `joint: video + action`; retain DreamDojo as `forward: action to video`.

### Issue 3: The VLA-class latency statement generalizes a Fast-WAM result to recent WAMs as a class

- **Slides:** `The architecture consolidated in 2026`; `Five architectures, one scoreboard`
- **Status / severity:** **MISMATCH — MAJOR (scope), not a numerical contradiction once scoped**
- **Slide says:** “at inference, recent WAMs skip video generation and act at VLA-class latency.” Later it says WAM inference is “3 to 4x slower.”
- **Sources say:** S27 reports **190 ms** for Fast-WAM, over **4x faster** than imagine-then-execute WAMs. S09 reports the default joint/inverse modes *with full video generation* at **590–800 ms** versus about **190 ms** for pi-0.5, hence **3–4x slower**. S25 says an *auxiliary-video-prediction* branch can be removed at inference; it does not say all recent WAMs do so.
- **Fix:** “Representation-only Fast-WAM skips video generation and reaches about 190 ms, VLA-class latency.” The later default-WAM `3 to 4x` caveat can then remain unchanged.

### Issue 4: DreamZero is not RoboArena rank 1 on the deck's own live source

- **Slide:** DreamZero full-bleed slide
- **Status / severity:** **MISMATCH — CRITICAL**
- **Slide says:** “Elo 1736 — RoboArena rank 1, vs 1609 for pi-0.5 (Aug 29).”
- **Source says:** S46's live API, last updated `2026-08-30T09:42:48Z`, returns `j2-vla` first at **1788**, `DreamZero` (`dreaming_zebra`) second at **1736**, and `pi05_droid` at **1609**. The weekly snapshots for Aug 24, Aug 17, and Aug 10 also place `j2-vla` above DreamZero.
- **Re-check:** the two Elo values on the slide are exact; only `rank 1` is false. This is not a rounding problem.
- **Fix:** remove `rank 1` and say “Elo 1736 vs 1609 for pi-0.5 (live snapshot, Aug 29),” or freeze and cite an immutable dated snapshot.

### Issue 5: “Zero robot episodes” drops the crucial pretraining qualifier

- **Slides:** Dyna-2 full-bleed slide; `The race is for hours`
- **Status / severity:** **MISMATCH — MAJOR**
- **Slide says:** “Dyna-2: a million hours of human video, zero robot episodes”; the table says “zero robot.”
- **Source says:** S01 says there was no robot data **during pretraining**, then says post-trained Dyna-2 policies use “a few hours of robot data” and one bottle-cap task uses about 10 minutes of teleoperation.
- **Fix:** “zero robot episodes in pretraining” in both places.

### Issue 8: The Dyna-2 graph uses the right values but names the wrong metric and overclaims a power-law fit

- **Slide:** `A1. Dyna-2: the scaling law in detail`
- **Status / severity:** **MISMATCH — MAJOR**
- **Slide says:** y-axis “14-task avg success, %”; bullet “One power law across four scales.” Values are **20 / 28 / 45 / 53** at **1k / 10k / 100k / 1M** hours.
- **Source says:** S01 calls these values the **mean normalized score** across 14 tasks, where each task's native metric is normalized to its attainable ceiling. It separately says held-out *prediction metrics* are well described by power laws. For the on-robot 20/28/45/53 series, it claims a monotonic scaling trend, not a fitted power law.
- **Re-check:** all four plotted values and all four hour rungs are exact.
- **Fix:** y-axis `14-task mean normalized score, %`; bullet `Monotonic on-robot scaling trend across four rungs, 1k to 1M hours`.

- **Slide says:** “Launched at Computex, Jun 2026.”
- **Source says:** S50 is dated **May 31, 2026** and says `NVIDIA GTC Taipei`; S28 was submitted **June 1, 2026**. The declared announcement does not call the event Computex.

### Issue 10: “Precision is the one axis data volume does not buy” overstates the precision law

- **Slide:** `The race is for hours, not parameters` footnote
- **Status / severity:** **MISMATCH — MINOR**
- **Slide says:** “precision is the one axis data volume does not buy.”
- **Source says:** S08 states that the required demonstrations grow **super-exponentially** as target precision approaches the system limit, summarized as `log(N) proportional to 1/(P-c)`. More data can buy precision; it becomes increasingly expensive and cannot pass the system's limit without changing the system.
- **Fix:** “precision becomes super-exponentially expensive as it approaches the system limit.”
