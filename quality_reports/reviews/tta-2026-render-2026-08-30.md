## Cross-deck findings

### Core diagram labels are below a row-30 threshold

- **Affected images:** 03, 09, 18, 21, 28, and 39.
- **Severity:** Medium.
- **Evidence:** the rendered schematics and charts are crisp and intact, but their essential labels are visibly much smaller than the 40 px slide text. The inline schematics use 13.5–19 px labels; the appendix scaling chart uses 13–14 px labels. From row 30, arrows and trends remain visible while the words that explain them do not.
- **Recommendation:** enlarge each diagram within the abundant white space and raise essential labels to roughly 26–30 px. Promote the smallest gold/gray annotation into normal slide text when it is the actual conclusion. Do not shrink surrounding prose; remove or split content if the larger diagram no longer fits.
- **Counterargument considered:** the presenter can narrate the diagrams. That is true, but these slides define VLA, world model, WAM, and the joint-model transition; a practitioner should be able to recover the mapping from the screen while listening.

### Small chip/footnote copy is acceptable only when genuinely secondary

- **Affected most materially:** images 06, 13, 22, and 40; lower-severity instances are identified below.
- **Severity:** Medium where the small line gives a number its units or states the slide's takeaway; Low where the headline and image remain self-explanatory.
- **Evidence:** chip descriptions are about 16.8 px and footnotes about 18 px. On image 13, for example, `17` is large but “Japanese industry leaders joined in July” is not; on image 06, the substantive “one hour of robot time buys one hour of one embodiment's data” appears in footnote-sized text.
- **Recommendation:** spell the meaning into the large label (`17 Japanese industry leaders`), or promote the takeaway to normal body type. Keep only provenance and licensing in the small caption/footnote tier.
- **Counterargument considered:** metadata is not meant to be read aloud or from the back row. Agreed; the report does not flag ordinary publisher/date/autonomy lines as defects.

### The blackout poster is intentional but weak as a static fallback

- **Affected image:** 24 / visible slide 25.
- **Severity:** Medium.
- **Evidence:** the caption explicitly says “Chopping through a blackout,” and the video manifest identifies the same deliberate darkness, so this is not a broken or missing poster. Nevertheless, the poster crop is effectively black in the screenshot: mean luminance is 6.4/255 and 99.6% of its pixels are below 25/255. In a PDF or paused classroom fallback, the robot and task are not recoverable from row 30.
- **Recommendation:** keep the live clip dark, but use a distinct print/poster fallback: an exposure-lifted still from the blackout segment, a before/blackout two-frame composite, or a clearly labeled visible reference inset beside the dark frame.
- **Counterargument considered:** brightening the live footage would erase the point. The recommendation applies to the static poster/PDF fallback, not the clip.
